Security Risk Assessment Report – Cloud-Based File-Sharing System

1. Executive Summary
   
This report assesses security risks in a medium-sized organisation’s cloud-based file-sharing system, which lets employees store, access, and share business documents online. Although cloud technology improves collaboration and accessibility, it can expose confidential information. Five key threats are phishing, malware, weak authentication, insider threats, and data breaches. Recommended protections include multi-factor authentication (MFA), encryption, security awareness training, and malware protection. These measures can safeguard sensitive information and strengthen overall security.

2. System/Scenario Overview
   
The organisation uses cloud platforms such as Microsoft OneDrive or Google Drive to store and share business documents. Employees access files via browsers, mobile apps, or company computers, and can upload, download, edit, and share them with colleagues and authorised partners. These platforms may hold sensitive employee, financial, and confidential business information. Internet access and multiple accounts create security risks: attackers who compromise accounts or exploit system weaknesses could access confidential data. Appropriate controls are essential to protect information and maintain business resources’ confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

3. Identified Threats and Vulnerabilities

3.1 Phishing Attacks

Phishing is a social engineering attack where cybercriminals trick employees into revealing login credentials through fraudulent messages. They may impersonate cloud providers and direct users to fake login pages. Stolen credentials can expose confidential files, enable information theft, or let attackers misuse accounts. Poor security awareness raises the risk.

3.2 Malware Infections

Employees may download or upload malicious files disguised as legitimate documents. Malware, including ransomware, can disrupt operations or encrypt critical files; access to shared cloud resources may spread the impact. Weak detection increases infection risk.

3.3 Weak Authentication and Unauthorised Access

Weak, reused passwords and no multi-factor authentication increase account-compromise risk. Attackers can exploit stolen credentials or automated guesses, while excessive permissions let employees access or alter files beyond their roles.

3.4 Insider Threats

Employees or contractors with legitimate access may intentionally misuse information or accidentally share confidential documents with unauthorised recipients. Poor access management and insufficient monitoring can make these incidents difficult to detect.

3.5 Data Breaches

Sensitive information may be exposed through compromised accounts, improperly configured sharing permissions, or insufficient encryption. A data breach may result in financial losses, reputational harm, and potential violations of data protection requirements.

4. Recommendations

Three key security measures are recommended.

4.1 Implement Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Require MFA for all user accounts, particularly administrators. This adds an extra verification step, reducing the likelihood of unauthorised access even if passwords are stolen.
   
4.2 Encrypt Data and Apply Access Controls: Encrypt files both at rest and during transmission using appropriate cryptographic protocols. Apply role-based access control and the principle of least privilege so users can access only the information required for their responsibilities. Regularly review sharing permissions and access logs.
   
4.3 Strengthen Security Awareness and Malware Protection: Train employees to spot phishing, suspicious links, and malicious attachments. Use reputable endpoint protection, scan for malware, update software, and test backups to recover from ransomware.
     
5. Conclusion
The assessment identifies phishing, malware, weak authentication, insider threats, and data breaches as significant risks to the organisation's cloud-based file-sharing system. Implementing multifactor authentication (MFA), encryption, appropriate access controls, and security awareness training, supported by malware protection, will reduce the likelihood and impact of security incidents. Regular monitoring, vulnerability assessments, and backup testing should complement these measures to maintain effective protection as threats evolve.
